# My notes

What I took from docs/findings.md, before writing the brief.

- The growth story is online. Of the 4,940 students Gies added from Fall 2017 to Fall 2026, 4,781 are in online graduate programs, while residential graduate enrollment fell from 1,068 to 680. A department head should read Gies's size as mostly an online-program story.

- Undergraduate enrollment is growing again, and the class is getting bigger. Fall 2026 brought 901 first-time freshmen, more than any fall since at least 2012. Accountancy juniors and seniors fell from 663 to 545 since Fall 2021, but Accountancy + Data Science, which didn't exist then, has 194; I'd want to know how many of those students would otherwise have been in Accountancy before calling it a decline.

- What surprised me most is where the rise in A grades happened. 17.5 of the 21.7-point rise in the A-range share from Fall 2015 to Fall 2025 happened inside courses taught in both years, not from students moving into higher-graded courses. That makes it a question for each course, and it is the kind of evidence an assurance-of-learning review could use to decide where to look first.

- DFW rates are too low to steer by. Only 1.4% of grades in Fall 2025 were D, F, or W. The share at C or below or withdrawing separates courses better, and five accounting and finance courses stand out on it. I'd bring those to their departments as questions about preparation and support, not as a ranking.

- Illinois keeps its freshmen better than most of the Big Ten (7th of 18) but awards a smaller share of its bachelor's degrees in business than all but four peers. I don't know yet why; the size of Illinois's other colleges is one candidate, and it would be worth checking before drawing anything from it.

- What I don't trust yet: the online and in-person comparison, because the two often serve different students; graded students per section, because the GPA data leaves out small sections; and the projection, which only matched a naive forecast in the backtest. I'd present all three with those caveats attached.

- The registration watch is the piece I'm most curious about. Until Tech Services approved the course-page pass, I could only see sections being added and removed; from Oct 28 I'll see which close first, which is the closest public data gets to where demand outruns the schedule.
